Understanding Blood Types

Blood types are determined by the presence or absence of certain antigens – substances that can trigger an immune response if they are foreign to the body. There are four main blood groups: A, B, AB, and O. Additionally, blood is classified by the Rhesus (Rh) factor – another antigen on the surface of red blood cells. If this antigen is present, the blood type is positive (+); if absent, it's negative (-).

A Type A Blood

  • Has A antigens on red blood cells
  • Has anti-B antibodies in plasma
  • Can donate to: A and AB
  • Can receive from: A and O
  • About 30% of population

B Type B Blood

  • Has B antigens on red blood cells
  • Has anti-A antibodies in plasma
  • Can donate to: B and AB
  • Can receive from: B and O
  • About 9% of population

AB Type AB Blood

  • Has both A and B antigens on red blood cells
  • Has no antibodies in plasma
  • Can donate to: AB only
  • Can receive from: A, B, AB, and O (Universal Receiver)
  • About 4% of population

O Type O Blood

  • Has no antigens on red blood cells
  • Has both anti-A and anti-B antibodies in plasma
  • Can donate to: A, B, AB, and O (Universal Donor)
  • Can receive from: O only
  • About 57% of population

Blood Donation Eligibility

Before donating blood, donors must meet certain eligibility criteria to ensure the safety of both the donor and the recipient. Here are some general requirements:

1

Age

Most donors must be at least 17 years old (16 with parental consent in some states) and there is no upper age limit for blood donation as long as you are well.

2

Weight

Donors must weigh at least 110 pounds (50 kg). Additional weight requirements apply for donors under 18 years old.

3

Health

You must be in good health and feeling well on the day of donation. You should not have a fever, sore throat, or other infections.

4

Hemoglobin Levels

Your hemoglobin level must be at least 12.5 g/dL for women and 13.0 g/dL for men. This will be checked before donation.

5

Time Between Donations

You must wait at least 8 weeks (56 days) between whole blood donations and 16 weeks (112 days) between double red cell donations.

6

Travel

Travel to certain countries may temporarily disqualify you from donating blood due to potential exposure to malaria or other diseases.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often can I donate blood?

For whole blood donations, you can donate once every 8 weeks (56 days). For double red cell donations, you can donate once every 16 weeks (112 days). For platelet donations, you can donate once every 7 days, up to 24 times per year.

Does donating blood hurt?

Most people report only a brief pinch when the needle is inserted. The actual donation process is usually painless. Our trained staff will make sure you're comfortable throughout the process.

How long does a blood donation take?

The entire process takes about one hour, though the actual blood donation only takes about 8-10 minutes. The rest of the time is spent on registration, a mini-physical, and refreshments after donation.

Is it safe to donate blood?

Yes, it is completely safe to donate blood. All equipment used for blood donation is sterile and used only once. There is no risk of contracting any disease by donating blood.

What should I eat before donating blood?

It's recommended to have a healthy, iron-rich meal before donating blood. Foods high in iron include red meat, spinach, beans, and fortified cereals. Also, drink plenty of water before your donation.
